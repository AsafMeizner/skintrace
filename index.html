Is it a change, or just the light?

Skin-analysis scores move with the window, the distance and the angle of the head. SkinTrace measures all three in both photos first, then either says what really changed or tells you exactly how to retake.

Compare two selfies

Both photos are read in memory by this app and are not saved. They stay on this machine unless you tick the YouCam box below, which sends them to YouCam. Use two photos of the same face, a JPEG or PNG under 10 MB. What it measures: capture conditions, and left-versus-right cheek redness. It does not judge your skin overall, and it is strict: expect to retake often.

What the light alone does

Measured by this engine on 12 photos of one face (six in front of a window, six with the window behind): 36 front-versus-behind pairs. Nothing about the skin changed between them.

36 of 36pairs show a false redness "change" if you simply compare the colour.
34 of 36pairs are refused as not comparable, with the reason.
2 of 36pairs still show a change in the left-versus-right cheek contrast.

The gate is strict: on held-out pairs taken in the same conditions it still refuses about 58% (spike/heldout-results.md), so expect retakes. Spike results, one person and one room: see spike/results.md. A sensitivity check that paints a known redness onto one cheek of real photos flags the right cheek and never the other one, with 2 false alarms in 120 pairs (spike/inject_results.md). It was not tested on a real treatment.

How it decides

  1. Fingerprint each photo from its pixels: face size and position, head turn and tilt from face landmarks, skin colour of each cheek, brightness and which side the light comes from.
  2. Measure the noise floor from repeats taken in the same conditions: how much each number moves by itself (SD, and the smallest change beyond that noise, MDC95).
  3. Gate: if any capture number differs by more than its noise floor, the pair is not comparable and the app says which one and how to retake.
  4. Cheek contrast: if comparable, it compares the change in the left cheek against the right cheek, so a shift of both cheeks together is reported separately, not as a one-cheek change. A difference larger than the noise floor is reported as a change on that cheek; it can still be a real change on both sides or an effect the gate cannot see.

YouCam's own scores move with the light

We sent 12 face crops of one person (six with the window in front, six behind, no treatment) to YouCam's skin analysis. Ten were scored; two backlit ones were rejected as too dark. Even among the accepted backlit photos, several scores moved far more than their own wobble between front-lit captures.
